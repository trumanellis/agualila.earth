1) Essence and Location

Água Lila is a land-based Temple and regenerative sanctuary in the mountains near São Pedro do Sul, embedded in a temperate Atlantic–Mediterranean climate with seasonal frost, abundant water, and strong solar exposure. The name evokes water (Água) as life-force and lilac/violet (Lila) as sacred play and cosmic creativity.

Core identity: a living Temple where spiritual practice, ecological regeneration, family life, and sovereign technology co-evolve.

⸻

2) Legal & Organizational Architecture

Água Lila functions as a physical Temple node within Temples of Refuge, a 508(c)(1)(A) network of Temples established under U.S. First Amendment religious protections.

Key legal characteristics:
	•	Religious, not commercial: organized for worship, ministry, education, and sacramental practice.
	•	Tax-exempt by definition (no IRS determination letter required for churches).
	•	Ministerial stewardship: land and operations are stewarded by ordained ministers.
	•	International footprint: while ToR is Utah-based, Água Lila represents an international Temple expression, with future pathways toward religious land-trust holding and stewardship assignments.

This structure intentionally prioritizes sovereignty, resilience, and continuity across generations.

⸻

3) Land, Ecology, and Regeneration

Água Lila is under active regenerative transformation, guided by syntropic and ecological design principles:
	•	Syntropic agroforestry replacing invasive eucalyptus with native and useful species
	•	Water intelligence: springs, slow streams, micro-swales, gravity-fed flows
	•	Sacred trees preserved as living altars
	•	Future food forests: citrus terraces, perennial polycultures, medicinal plants

The land is treated not as property but as a conscious participant—a co-teacher in ceremony and daily life.

⸻

4) Architecture & Infrastructure (Off-Grid by Design)

Água Lila is being built as a low-energy, off-grid Temple habitat, including:
	•	Passive-solar structures with thermal mass
	•	Greenhouses and biodomes (food + ceremony)
	•	Outdoor Temple spaces with circular geometry
	•	Composting and vermifilter sanitation systems
	•	Micro-hydro, solar, and battery-based energy stacks
	•	Sacred interior Temple room with ritual wall orientation and sound design

The architecture supports ritual, family life, guests, and long-duration stays, not tourism.
